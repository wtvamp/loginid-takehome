## Why this refinement corrects the backlog's own framing before restating criteria

`backlog.md`'s Story 6 description names the enforcement site as "a retention policy on the log sink 04 already operates (Loki or equivalent)" and its own acceptance criteria assume this track can simply "configure the sink's retention setting to that window." **Both assumptions are wrong, confirmed by Amber's read-only inventory, not assumed correct at design time:**

- **There is no Loki.** The cluster runs Filebeat (Helm DaemonSet, not an ECK `Beat` CR) shipping every namespace's stdout — this project's included, already, at no cost to us — into a single shared Elasticsearch data stream, `filebeat-9.4.4`, owned by the `logging` namespace's operators, not by this track.
- **Note: Amber's Filebeat `health`-substring `drop_event` fix (in progress, shared ConfigMap) does not change this story's ILM/retention question at all.** That fix addresses which log lines make it into the stream in the first place (LT-49's metrics-sink concern, not this story's) — it has zero bearing on how long entries that do land in `filebeat-9.4.4` are retained once there, which is this story's entire subject. Noted explicitly so this fix isn't later mistaken for progress on LT-20.
- **04 does not operate this sink.** `filebeat-9.4.4` is configured by a ConfigMap (`filebeat-filebeat-daemonset-config`) and backed by an Elasticsearch cluster this project's CI deployer has zero access to — no Kubernetes RBAC grant reaches Elasticsearch's own object model at all (an ES index-lifecycle-management, ILM, policy is a cluster-level object in a different system, not a Kubernetes CRD this namespace's `Role` could ever cover).
- **The retention window likely can't be scoped to just this project's data on the license the cluster runs — extrapolated from Amber's inventory, not a claim she stated directly, so flagged as such.** Amber's inventory confirms the ES license is **Basic** and that document-level security (the feature that would let a *read role* scope to only `kubernetes.namespace: loginid-takehome`) is a Platinum/Enterprise feature — stated for read access specifically. She did not separately address whether ILM/retention policies carry the same per-document restriction; this refinement infers they do, on the reasonable but unconfirmed assumption that ILM operates at the same index/stream granularity DLS would otherwise need to narrow. **This inference itself is the first thing to confirm with whoever owns `logging`**, not something to treat as already established the way the read-role limitation is.

This means Story 6 as originally scoped — "this story configures the sink's retention setting to that window once it's a number" — describes an action this track cannot actually take, on infrastructure this track does not own, at a granularity the platform's own license doesn't support. Per the PM's routing: **this story's job is to say what retention it needs and who applies it, not to pretend this namespace can apply it itself.**

## 05 ruling — the audit-log retention window

**≥ 90 days, and it is a floor rather than a ceiling.** That distinction is the ruling, not a caveat on it: every other number in `05-data-ops/pii-governance.md` is a **maximum** driven by minimization — data kept no longer than necessary — and this one is a **minimum** driven by incident review. Applying minimization thinking to an audit trail deletes the evidence, so the two kinds of number must not be read as the same kind. The audit events carry no PII payload under the never-log discipline, and the subject identifiers they do carry (profile UUIDs, caller client_ids) stop being linkable to a person once the profile they name is deleted — so nothing pushes this number *down*, and the only question is how far up.

**Why 90 and not a year.** It is short specifically because the durable proof-of-deletion lives in the `deletion_log` **table**, not in this stream. The stream is corroborating detail — who called what, when — and 90 days covers the ordinary incident-review horizon for that. Had the table not existed, the stream would be primary evidence and the floor would be materially longer.

**How it has to be expressed, given the sink.** The audit events share a filebeat data stream with other logs on a Basic licence, so per-source retention is not separable. The requirement is therefore on the *stream's minimum*: it may retain longer for other reasons, it must not retain less. **05 states the floor; 04 owns whether and how it is configured**, which is why this story closes as a stated requirement with a named owner rather than as applied configuration.

**Three revisit triggers, each with what it means:**

1. **`deletion_log`'s own window is ruled shorter than 90 days.** The 90 depends on that table being the durable record; if the table becomes more perishable than the stream, the stream becomes primary evidence and the floor must rise to cover what the table no longer does.
2. **Any contractual or regulatory retention commitment** — a PCI-style one-year obligation, a customer contract. The floor stops being an engineering judgment and becomes a minimum someone is accountable for.
3. **The never-log discipline breaks.** The dangerous one. If PII reaches the logs, the stream acquires a *minimum* from incident review and a *maximum* from storage limitation at the same time — **two constraints that can conflict, with no correct configuration available.** That makes the never-log list load-bearing for this ruling rather than merely good hygiene: it is the thing keeping this a one-sided constraint.
