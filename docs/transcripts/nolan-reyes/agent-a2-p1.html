16	### 1. What happens to the vendor `access_token` once retrieved
17	
18	- **Never logged.** The `/auth` request body contains a vendor password; the response contains a bearer token. That's superseded by F3: our own `/auth` and `/identity` are genuinely separate calls from our caller's perspective (matching the assignment's fixed two-endpoint contract), and it is the **caller** (`api-service`, on the onboarding flow's behalf) that calls our `/auth`, receives the vendor token, and presents it as a header on the following `/identity` call. 42	### 4. Failure handling as a security control, not just reliability
43	
44	- **Vendor auth failures must not leak which part of the credential was wrong** (username vs. password) in whatever this connector surfaces upward — same user-enumeration concern as the threat model's credential-stuffing row, now facing outward at whoever calls our `/auth` passthrough.
45	- **Retry/backoff on vendor calls** should be rate-limited on our side independent of the vendor's own limits, so a compromised or careless caller can't use our connector to hammer the vendor (which is both an availability risk to us and a way to get our connector's vendor credentials rate-limited or banned).
46	- **Circuit-breaking**: if the vendor is failing or behaving anomalously (e.g., returning tokens for identities that don't match the request), fail closed — do not fall back to a cached or stale identity result silently, since that risks serving stale PII as if freshly verified.
47	- **Timing side-channel on `/identity`'s fail-closed error, partially mitigated, accepted residual (found by Tobias Lindqvist during LT-41 refinement, ruled 2026-09-13).** The identical-error requirement for "no token" / "bad token" / "vendor rejected it" (§1) covers response *body and status code*, but the three paths have inherently different latencies: no-token and bad-token fail locally (microseconds), vendor-rejected requires an actual round-trip to the third-party IDP (tens–hundreds of ms, and variable — the vendor's latency is not under our control). An attacker can distinguish "malformed/missing token" from "well-formed enough to reach the vendor" via timing alone, regardless of identical bodies.
48	
49	  **This is a materially lower-severity leak than the classic timing side-channel this track has otherwise guarded against (e.g., password/account-existence timing, which this design closes by always running the same fixed-cost hash regardless of outcome).** What's disclosed here is only that a presented string has a shape that reached the vendor — not whether any specific identity or account exists, not PII, and not which part of a credential was wrong (§4's existing rule already normalizes vendor-rejection reasons). It's recon on token *shape*, not on any subject's identity.
50	
51	  **Mitigation applied, not full closure: a response-time floor (~100ms, well under any `context.WithTimeout` budget in the call chain, so it doesn't interact with the timeout requirements elsewhere) applied uniformly across all three failure branches** — padding the fast local-fail paths up to roughly the floor narrows the trivially-observable microseconds-vs-hundreds-of-ms gap into something requiring statistical analysis across many requests rather than one request telling an attacker everything instantly. **Full closure is not pursued and is accepted as a residual**, because the vendor round-trip's latency *distribution* (not just its average) varies with real-world network and vendor conditions in ways a fixed floor cannot fully mask — unlike a local hash computation, which this design can and does make constant-time-equivalent regardless of outcome, a third-party network call's tail latency isn't ours to control. Closing this further would mean either delaying every request to match the vendor's worst observed latency (a real availability cost for a low-value information leak) or removing the network call from the timing entirely (impossible — we must actually ask the vendor). Accepted for this system on the grounds that the disclosed information is low-value token-shape recon, not identity or PII exposure, and the cost of full closure is disproportionate to that value.
